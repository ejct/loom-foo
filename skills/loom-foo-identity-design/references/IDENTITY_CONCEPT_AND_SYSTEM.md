# Identity concept and system coherence

## From brand purpose to recognizable grammar

Find what the identity should make perceivable and memorable within its actual category. Separate researched customer/category evidence from speculative positioning; a pleasing logo does not prove market distinctiveness. When comparing directions, vary underlying mechanisms, not only hue and surface polish.

## Identity kit — scoped, not mandatory full deliverable

Possible elements: symbol, wordmark, lockup hierarchy, horizontal/stacked/icon-only variants, one-color/reversed, primary/secondary palette, type roles and glyph fallback, imagery and graphic device, spacing/clear-space, key applications. Include only requested or necessary elements.

A mark should remain optically coherent at realistic use sizes; a brand may require a simplified small-format signature. Demonstrate actual applications rather than an unconstrained presentation board. Avoid relying on copyrighted reference logos, proprietary font bytes or invented market/legal claims.

## Distinguish roles

Identity defines *what* visual relationships belong to the brand. Typography refines type craft; Icon Design builds functional or launcher symbols; Design Systems maps approved identity values to reusable product tokens and components; Graphic Design composes campaign artifacts. One integrated task can need multiple methods without mandatory skill chaining.
