# English editorial variants — en-US and en-GB · v0.1

This is a *selective source and variation guide*, not a complete grammar engine, universal house style, or proof that a dictionary was consulted. Choose US, UK or another English variety from the user's audience and request. Mixed/global English is legitimate; do not arbitrarily force all text to either US or UK.

## Typical variation classes

- Regional spellings such as `color`/`colour`, `organize`/`organise` (note that `-ize` can be correct in British Oxford usage), `center`/`centre`; check actual word and requested house style before calling an alternative an error.
- Punctuation inside quotation marks, use of single/double quotes and serial comma vary with style guide and publisher, not solely a national grammar rule.
- Technical capitalization, API tokens and product naming may be controlled by project terminology, not ordinary spelling conventions.
- Grammar/idiom variation (e.g. collective nouns) may be contextual; do not mislabel all choices as universal violations.
- In translated or localized business documents, numbers, dates, monetary values, units and legal promises are sensitive. Preserve actual amounts/IDs and ask before changing jurisdiction-specific meaning.

## Source pointers (verify concrete entries when consequential)

- Oxford English Dictionary: https://www.oed.com/ — historical and current meanings/variants, subscription may be needed.
- Cambridge Dictionary: https://dictionary.cambridge.org/ — usage and British/American variants.
- Merriam-Webster Dictionary: https://www.merriam-webster.com/ — American usage and spelling.
- UK Government content style: https://www.gov.uk/guidance/style-guide — relevant **only** where gov.uk house style is actually applicable, not a universal British norm.

A link here is **not a claim of retrieval** in the current task. Sources can disagree by context or editorial convention. Distinguish a grammatical error, a dictionary-accepted variant, a publisher's house style and an author's intentional register. For other English varieties (en-AU, en-CA, etc.) seek appropriate sources or mark unresolved cases instead of defaulting silently to US/UK.
