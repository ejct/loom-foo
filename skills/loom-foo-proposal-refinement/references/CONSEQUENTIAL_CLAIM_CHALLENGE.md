# Consequential claim challenge

For consequential claims only. Original detailed guidance retained verbatim below.

## 8. Challenge consequential claims when warranted

On a consequential proposal, focus first on the **load-bearing claim** that could reverse the recommendation if false. Identify its exact scope, what observation would genuinely contradict it, and the cheapest credible authorized direct check (source, negative example, small probe, or comparison). Try to obtain counterevidence instead of relying only on another model's speculation. Check that purported counterevidence is relevant, authentic and independent; a critic can be wrong. Distinguish unsupported from contradicted. If no useful check is currently available, bound the uncertainty and continue reversible authorized work instead of manufacturing a validation stage. No finding is a valid outcome.

For deep reviews, treat the exact proposal as a bounded subject and inspect material risks rather than running every lens automatically. Material lenses include:

- incorrect problem framing;
- unsupported assumptions;
- contradictions and missing states;
- conflicts with accepted decisions or current authority;
- hidden dependencies and coupling;
- duplicated primitives;
- premature abstraction;
- simpler alternatives missed by construction;
- weak or padded option comparisons;
- UX/product failure modes;
- operational/reliability failure modes;
- security/privacy implications when applicable;
- migration/adoption hazards;
- observability and validation gaps;
- irreversible decisions or hidden lock-in;
- scope creep and accidental complexity;
- cases where the proposal optimizes the wrong objective;
- hidden context that prevents the artifact from standing on its own sufficiently for its intended reader.

Repeat or rotate lenses only if the first challenge found a material unresolved weakness or new evidence changes the decision. Do not manufacture findings to justify another pass.

### Reviewer independence

Use separate context, agent, model invocation, or evaluator when the runtime genuinely supports it and the added separation is proportionate to the decision risk.

If the same model/context performs the review, call it a **fresh adversarial pass** or **adversarial self-review**, not an independent audit.

Never fabricate evaluator independence.
