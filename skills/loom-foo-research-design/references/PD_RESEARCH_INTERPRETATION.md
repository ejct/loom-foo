## 11. Define the interpretation contract

Before execution, state enough to distinguish:
- evidence that supports the intended direction/claim;
- evidence that weakens or refutes it (**not merely absence of support**);
- evidence that remains inconclusive;
- claims the study cannot license even after a positive result.

Preserve important distinctions such as:
- verification ≠ validation;
- technical feasibility ≠ user value;
- reproducibility ≠ replicability ≠ generalizability;
- correlation ≠ causation;
- no detected difference ≠ meaningful equivalence;
- one harness result ≠ model-level capability independent of the harness.
## 12. Define stop and adaptation rules

For any study where optional stopping or mid-run changes could bias interpretation, state:
- when enough evidence has been collected;
- when the plan should expand or repeat;
- what would trigger redesign;
- which adaptations are allowed without invalidating the original claim.

Do not silently change treatment, cases, metrics, or stopping criteria after outcomes are visible and keep calling the result confirmatory.
## 13. Minimize the plan

Remove any procedure that does not materially protect:
- decision relevance;
- interpretability;
- claim validity;
- required reproducibility;
- safety / authority;
- material cost or risk.

A small reversible question should produce a small plan—or no separate plan at all when a direct check is sufficient. A challenge is not an automatic research commission, approval gate, permanent status or request for independent evaluators.

Prompt length, number of methods, number of metrics, and number of runs are not quality targets.
## 14. Emit an Evidence Plan

Use the narrowest form that remains execution-ready.

The output should make these dimensions recoverable when material:
- `status`;
- decision / intended use;
- current uncertainty;
- claim / live alternatives;
- evidence-value judgment;
- discriminating evidence / identifiability;
- method family;
- exact subject / population / environment;
- comparator / controls when needed;
- measures / oracles and known limits;
- material confounds / validity hazards;
- interpretation contract;
- stop / adaptation rule;
- execution handoff;
- claim limits.

Do not force unused headings into trivial plans.
