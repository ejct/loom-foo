# Historical translation and simulated speech

A historical language is not merely an old spelling or register. Define source/target variety, period/range, region, genre, medium (speech/writing), speaker/audience roles, purpose and attested source status when relevant. Unknown fields remain unknown; avoid generic “ancient mindset”. A speaker's likely language and the language of the surviving record are distinct.

## Choose the actual operation

- Translate an attested historical text: preserve its edition, reading, textual variants, source locators and original uncertainty. A manuscript reading, normalized text, and translation are separate.
- Reconstruct a possible earlier expression behind a later text: record evidence and alternative reconstructions; do not imply recovery of the unique original. Later translations are not proof of original wording.
- Simulate an arbitrary modern text in a historical variety or communicative setting: label the whole exercise simulated. Even attested component words do not make a newly composed sentence historical testimony. A modern concept may need paraphrase, a deliberate anachronism or an unresolved segment. Do not invent an ancient institution, idiom or belief to fill a gap.

## Evidence method

Prefer dated, region-appropriate primary texts and critical editions, historical grammar/dialect dictionaries and corpus citations. Check attestation of the actual sense and form, not just existence of the lemma; separate dated text from manuscript date, transliteration from reconstruction, and corpus genre from spoken register. A later or neighboring dialect is evidence by analogy, not direct attestation. Inspect each claimed citation; never generate fictional attestations or fabricate missing words. If a language is poorly supported, provide a gloss/semantic paraphrase or stop the unsupported segment rather than dress guesses in ancient script.

Use explicit evidence status at the level it supports: `attested` (exact form/reading supported), `reconstructed` (evidence-constrained proposal), `simulated` (new composition), `unsupported` (insufficient basis). Whole-text and individual-form status may differ. Add uncertainty and evidence locators where relevant. Do not use arbitrary confidence percentages. Consult specialist review for consequential historical claims.

## Profiles and sources (starting points, not pre-verified attestations)

- Aramaic: Comprehensive Aramaic Lexicon, https://cal.huc.edu/ and https://cal.huc.edu/newtextmenu.html (dialect/text selection; unfinished lexicon, not direct access to unrecorded first-century speech).
- Greek/Latin: Scaife ATLAS https://atlas.perseus.tufts.edu/ (text and lemma lookup; check edition, date and genre).
- Biblical languages: Yale resource guide https://guides.library.yale.edu/electronic_religion/languages (discovery, not primary evidence).

For a first-century Galilean speech reconstruction, do not substitute Syriac or Biblical Aramaic without labeling the proxy and its limits. For a Bible transmission route, verify each edition's actual textual source(s): Vulgate, Wycliffite and King James traditions need not form one serial chain. Oral performance, oral transmission, composition, textual revision, translation and modern interpretation are distinct transition kinds. Verify the specific work rather than a sweeping historical lineage. If the caller requests a hypothetical route, it may be used as such, with no claim of historical descent.
