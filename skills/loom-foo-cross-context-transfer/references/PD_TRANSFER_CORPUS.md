## 6. Use bounded completeness claims

- internal corpus: `EXHAUSTIVE_IN_SCOPE`
- public repository: `DECLARED_SURFACE_COMPLETE`
- research: `SEARCH_PROTOCOL_COMPLETE`
- other source set: state exact scope/basis

Never claim global exhaustiveness.

Keep three judgments distinct:

- **Corpus Coverage** — how completely the declared in-scope artifacts were reviewed.
- **Discovery Coverage** — which materially relevant solution/source categories and independent discovery channels were explored; this is bounded and may expose unknown gaps.
- **Decision Sufficiency** — whether current transferable knowledge is enough for the target's next practical choice, including when useful gaps remain.

`EXHAUSTIVE_IN_SCOPE` or `SEARCH_PROTOCOL_COMPLETE` establishes completion only against the declared corpus or protocol; neither proves discovery breadth or global absence of alternatives. Repetition of findings within one channel alone does not establish discovery sufficiency. An incomplete broader landscape can still support a reversible, bounded next step. Do not demand exhaustive discovery when it would not materially change the owner's decision.

If a material source remains partial/unavailable, lower the claim or use `BLOCKED` only when that source is necessary for the intended transfer claim; otherwise bound the conclusion and name the gap.
## 8. Close the material-reference frontier

Follow newly discovered references only when they can materially change authority, rationale, evidence, supersession, failure interpretation, target relevance, or transfer conclusion.

Do not recursively follow every citation/link.

For open-ended discovery, material newly encountered categories and mechanisms can justify searching outside the initial source list. Prefer independent routes (e.g., implementations, product practice, research, user experience, failure history) when selection bias could hide a better option. Deeper reading should concentrate on candidate mechanisms that could change target fit. Stop expanding when the likely value of further search is lower than making the next reversible decision; record meaningful unexplored directions. This supplements the bounded corpus contract without converting every transfer task into a landscape census.
## 9. Task-conditioned review of every in-scope artifact

For each artifact ask:

- what problem/force does it reveal?
- what decisions/mechanisms matter?
- what evidence supports them?
- what failed/rejected/superseded/unvalidated knowledge matters?
- what could change the transfer conclusion?
- is it materially relevant to this task?

"Opened" is not "considered".
## 10. Resolve contradictions and temporal authority

Reconcile current vs old, proposal vs accepted decision, design vs implementation, claim vs experiment, success vs incident/audit, and source-vs-source conflicts.

Relations:

`SUPPORTS | CONTRADICTS | SUPERSEDES | FAILED_IN_PRACTICE | CONTEXTUAL_VARIANT | UNVALIDATED`

Negative knowledge is first-class.
