# Evidence-grounded project / UX case studies · v0.1

A case study is an evidence-bearing account of actual work. The author may use chronology, decision points, comparative alternatives or a thematic arc; **do not impose a process diagram**. Aim to make the reader understand the problem, the practitioner's contribution, the hard choices and their consequences.

## Factual distinctions to preserve

- Observation vs interpretation vs preference vs aspiration.
- User research observation vs designer assumption vs product hypothesis.
- Proposed concept vs implemented prototype vs released feature vs measured production outcome.
- Individual design role vs team decision vs client direction.
- Before/after comparison where the baseline is documented; no invented uplift or metrics.
- Actual constraints and evidence vs convenient retrospective narrative.

## Possible argument spine (not mandatory)

Context → tension or specific user/business problem → constraints and evidence → options considered → decision and trade-off → implementation/state of delivery → observed consequences and unknowns.

If outcomes are unknown, describe what was delivered and how it can be evaluated rather than fabricating impact. If the project ended at exploration/prototype, that can be the honest result.

## Source and privacy

Where available, link to actual artifacts, anonymized research material and project documents with the right disclosure rights. Do not import private customer names, screenshots, metrics or conversations into distributable skill examples. Public cases must use synthetic or permission-cleared material.

## Example

Weak: "We revolutionized onboarding and raised activation by 40%." (no evidence supplied)

Defensible from a prototype-only source: "I mapped the onboarding decision points, compared two information flows, and developed a prototype for evaluation. No post-launch activation data was available." 

This is a **synthetic illustration**, not a report about a real person or project.
