# Narrative architecture methods · v0.1

Load only for substantive architecture, structural review or complex sequencing. These are heuristics, not universal phases, UI components or authoring requirements.

## Frame by audience job

- Decision brief: decision/context → options and constraints → supporting evidence → trade-offs → recommendation/remaining uncertainty. Put the decision early when the reader needs speed; don't force suspense.
- Explanation/technical article: motivating question → necessary concepts/dependencies → mechanism → edge case → implications. Reference diagrams and code as evidence, not decorative elements.
- Research/evidence report: question and methods → observed findings → interpretation and limitations → implications; do not disguise a hypothesis as a result.
- Narrative essay: observation or tension → development → perspective shift or qualified conclusion; voices and ambiguity may legitimately remain unresolved.
- Proposal: desired change → actual current condition → viable mechanisms → evidence and costs → next decision; avoid promotional claims unsupported by the record.
- Tutorial: prerequisites → steps with expected outcomes → checks/recovery; chronology and setup constraints may outrank rhetorical drama.

## Claim–support ledger (optional)

For consequential arguments, use a small in-memory map:

| Element | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | What is asserted? Is it descriptive, predictive or normative? |
| Evidence | What exact source supports it; what was actually observed? |
| Inference | Which reasoning connects evidence to claim? What else could explain it? |
| Boundary | What remains unknown, contested or untested? |
| Placement | When must the reader learn this to understand the next section? |

A missing source is a gap, not permission to invent a reference. A credible argument may be comparative rather than chronological. Preserve distinctions between "we saw", "we inferred", "we decided" and "the product achieved".

## Structural operations

- **Compress:** reduce repeated context while preserving claims, sources, qualifications and causal logic.
- **Expand:** recover a missing premise or decision-relevant detail from actual sources; ask for missing evidence only if necessary.
- **Reorder:** reorder by reader dependency, not by arbitrary dramatic peak. For a before/after map, link each old section to a new destination.
- **Branch:** if multiple audiences need different depth, suggest optional sections/appendices; avoid hiding critical limitations.
- **Bridge:** propose a transition using existing evidence; use "why this follows" rather than invented causal assertions.
- **Counterbalance:** surface strongest material alternative/limitation where one-sided presentation could mislead.

## Critique lenses

Does the opening establish a real reader question? Does each section contribute something different? Is the interpretation supported? Are claims about people and products attributed? Are alternative explanations represented? Are the times, actors and sequence correct? What can be cut without losing necessary meaning? Is the ending consistent with the evidence rather than merely satisfying a story formula?

Do not override explicit creative goals, cultural narratives, nonlinear writing or deliberately withheld information. Structure is an editorial hypothesis until supported by reader response or task evidence.
