# Criteria and Authority

## Input classes

Keep these distinct:
- evidence assessment;
- hard constraint/requirement;
- owner preference/value;
- working assumption;
- authority/policy;
- recommendation.

## Hard constraints

Apply non-negotiable constraints before soft preferences. A hard violation cannot be offset by score or preference.

## Criteria provenance

For each material decision criterion, preserve where it came from. If a criterion is not authorized or owned, do not silently invent it.

## Owner criteria

Use `NEED_OWNER_CRITERION` when an unresolved normative choice materially changes the recommendation and only the owner can supply it.

Examples:
- time-to-market vs robustness;
- lower cost vs lower operational risk;
- reversibility vs peak performance;
- privacy vs convenience.

## Weighting

Weighted scoring is optional. Use it only when the weights and scale are legitimate and useful. Never invent weights for cosmetic objectivity.

## Authority

Analysis can recommend. Acceptance/canonicalization/implementation/release/spend remain separate authority transitions.
