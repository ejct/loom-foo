## 4. Consider materially different routes, then select one

Generate only plausible alternatives that could change the choice, such as:

- direct completion vs staged release;
- low-cost exploratory probe vs immediate build;
- sequential work vs independently parallel branches;
- reuse/partner/integrate vs build in-house;
- unblocking existing work vs switching to a fallback route.

Compare with grounded criteria: goal fidelity, value of next outcome, prerequisite availability, time/resource demand, operational complexity, reversibility, material failure modes, uncertainty and authority. Prefer qualitative trade-offs over fabricated scores. For a fixed user preference, do not treat a disfavored path as equally attractive without counterevidence.

When the **project route itself** has a consequential open choice, first frame the owner outcome and plausible local routes without treating any external example as the default. Consult external prior art **conditionally**, by underlying outcome and alternative mechanism rather than the favorite route. Reconcile what it changes about feasibility, route choice or the framing; state explicitly when nothing material changes. Verify necessary standards, safety limits, access and external facts immediately when they control the problem—do not postpone factual checks just to preserve independence. Do not impose a rigid universal sequence or turn every plan into a discovery campaign. A specialized proposal, architecture, research or skill-engineering workflow owns deeper design-space exploration when that is the real requested artifact.

Choose a **recommended route**, not an indiscriminate menu. Explain why the runner-up is inferior under current constraints and what finding would reverse the preference. If evidence cannot discriminate and the next step is cheap and reversible, choose the smallest useful probe instead of an extended theoretical plan.

**Feasibility guard:** before recommending a route, identify any decisive missing prerequisite, impossible resource assumption or unauthorized action that makes it unusable. If **no feasible route** exists within verified current constraints, say so plainly; preserve the original outcome, distinguish a partial fallback from its fulfillment, identify the smallest decision/resource/authority change that could reopen a route, and continue genuinely independent authorized work, if any remains. Never create a fictional milestone or a new validation gate to conceal infeasibility.

Never substitute an adjacent objective (benchmarking, methodological proof, exhaustive auditing, perfect documentation, tool building) for the owner's desired functional result. Necessary correctness, safety, source fidelity and external-action gates remain mandatory.
